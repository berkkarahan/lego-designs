# Creative conversation

The user controls intent; the agent supplies imagination and technical judgment. Avoid turning the conversation into an intake form or requiring approval of every routine engineering choice.

## Directed brief
Extract the defining constraints, theme and desired play. Check inventory affordances, then offer creative directions only where a meaningful choice exists. A tracked tank could prioritize a suspension experiment, a compact scout or a transformable machine; do not force those examples or any fixed list on other prompts.

Ask only about consequential unknowns: ambiguous terms, incompatible goals, scale that changes feasibility, or the user's preferred tradeoff. Do not ask where inventory files live or which listed parts are owned.

## Open-ended / surprise
Use stock, existing designs and interesting mechanisms as inspiration. Pitch contrasting experiences and silhouettes rather than cosmetic variations. Explain what makes each concept fun and what its uncertain parts would require. Recommend one, then wait for selection or steering before detailed construction.

## Steering and continuation
Incorporate "make it smaller", "more unusual", "use the other motor", or a new theme into the active design. Revisit parts and mechanism choices. If intent changes completely, ask whether to keep the prototype as a separate design only when folder identity is unclear; never overwrite it silently.

Offer inventory-compatible alternatives when a connection or mechanism does not work on paper. Imagination is broad; evidence sets what can be promised. Capture the chosen concept, important preferences, open questions and next steps in the design README instead of relying on conversation history.
